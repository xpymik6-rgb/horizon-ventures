Every large wiki hides hundreds of pages nobody has touched in years: outdated policies, dead projects, instructions for systems long gone. They are invisible to search, because nobody remembers what to search for.

What Confluence already does, and where this fits

Worth saying plainly, because you will find it yourself in ten minutes and we would rather you heard it from us. Confluence Cloud has two native answers here, and for a lot of teams they are enough.

The content manager. Every space has one, in space settings, with a Last active filter that defaults to 180 days: content nobody has viewed, commented on or updated in that time. Free, native, and exactly the right question. Three limits: it is an administrator's screen, so the people who wrote those pages never see the list; acting on the results in bulk needs Premium or Enterprise; and it works one space at a time.

The filter by label macro. Stronger than its name suggests. Twelve filters, including space, title, text, creator, contributor and subpages, all picked from a form rather than written as a query. What it cannot express is a relative period: its date filters return items last updated on a date, so "not touched in six months" has no equivalent, and a list pinned to a fixed date quietly stops meaning what its heading says a month later.

Where this app sits. In the gap between them. A relative period like "not updated in 180 days", on an ordinary page any reader can open, across several spaces at once, refreshing itself, on any plan. If your case is one space and one clean-up, use the content manager: it is free, it is native, and it will outlive us. Questions people ask

Why not just use the Confluence content manager?

For a one-off clean-up of a single space, use it. It is free, native, and its Last active filter asks exactly the right question. Three things it does not do: it lives in space settings, so only administrators see the list; bulk actions on the results require Premium or Enterprise; and it works one space at a time. This app puts the same kind of list on an ordinary page, across several spaces, on any plan.

Can the filter by label macro do this?

Almost. It accepts twelve filters from a form, including space, title, text, creator, contributor and subpages, so it covers far more than its name suggests. What it cannot express is a relative period: its date filters return items last updated on a given date, not items untouched for six months. A list built on a fixed date stops meaning what its heading says a month later.

Do I need to know CQL?

No. Conditions are chosen from dropdowns, and the app states in plain English what the list will contain before you save it. If you do know CQL, there is a manual mode where you write the query yourself.

Does the list update itself?

Yes, it is a live list rather than a snapshot. When somebody finally updates a forgotten page, that page leaves the list on its own, and nobody has to maintain a page of links.

Where does my content go?

Nowhere. The app runs on Atlassian infrastructure under the Runs on Atlassian programme. Pages are read inside your own Confluence site, we store nothing, and nothing leaves your site.
