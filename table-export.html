A whole space, one workbook

Other exporters give you the table you are looking at. This one walks the entire space, collects every table it finds and hands you a single Excel workbook — each table labelled with the page it came from. That last part matters: without page names, fifty tables in one file are unusable.

It reads only pages you are allowed to open. The request runs as you, not as the app.

Merged cells stay where they belong

A cell spanning two columns leaves a hole in a naive export, and everything after it slides one column left. On a budget table that means numbers landing under the wrong heading — an error nobody spots until it costs something.

Here merged cells are unrolled, so every row comes out the same width. Cells with several paragraphs keep their line breaks. Pick comma or semicolon, depending on what your Excel expects.

Nothing leaves your site

The page is read with your permissions, the file is built inside your browser, and your browser hands it to you. There is no server of ours in the path — there is no server of ours at all. The app carries the Runs on Atlassian badge, which means Atlassian verifies this at the platform level rather than taking our word.

For payroll registries and HR tables this is usually the whole argument.

Pricing

Free for teams up to 10 people. Above that, $1.50 per user per month, billed by Atlassian with your other apps.

A 25-person team pays $37.50 a month. Cancel from your Atlassian admin at any time.

Exporting one table by hand takes a minute. Exporting forty of them, from pages scattered across a space, takes an afternoon — and has to be redone next quarter. That is what you are paying for.
