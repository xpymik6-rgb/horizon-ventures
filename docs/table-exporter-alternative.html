Looking for a Table Exporter alternative

Table Exporter for Confluence turns tables on a page into CSV and Excel files. It runs on a platform Atlassian stops supporting on 31 January 2027. Here is how to keep exporting, free routes first.

Short answer. Table Exporter for Confluence is a Connect app with around 630 installations, last released on 31 January 2024. Atlassian ends support for Connect on 31 January 2027. Its vendor, Narva Software, is active and publishes other apps on Forge, so first check whether this one gets a Forge version. If it does not: for an occasional table, copy and paste into Excel works; for exports that are part of a regular process, Table Filter from Stiltsoft (if you already own it) or Table Export are the Forge replacements.

What is happening to Table Exporter

Connect apps run on their vendor's servers and talk to your site over the network; Forge apps run inside Atlassian's own infrastructure. Since March 2026 Connect apps cannot ship updates, and Atlassian moved the end of support from December 2026 to 31 January 2027, warning that breakages will increase afterwards.

This case is less clear than most abandoned apps. The vendor, formerly Bitwelt and now Narva Software, maintains several Forge apps and released updates to them in 2026. That is a good sign. What decides it is the listing itself: a release dated 2026 and the "Runs on Atlassian" badge mean the exporter has moved. As of October 2026 it shows neither. A second Connect app with a similar name, Table Exporter To CSV/Excel for Confluence, has not been updated since 2022.

What Confluence does natively

There is no built-in export for a single table. Confluence exports whole pages to PDF and Word, and space exports to CSV and XML carry page content, not tables as rows. The free routes that genuinely work:

  • Copy and paste. Select the table, copy, and in Excel use Paste Special as text, so Confluence's styling stays behind. For a simple rectangular table, this is the whole answer.
  • Export the page to Word, then copy. A detour, but Word handles merged cells more predictably than the browser clipboard. Worth it for one complicated table.
  • The REST API. The only free route that scales to many pages, if someone can script it. Tables produced by macros are stored as the macro, not as rows, so a script finds nothing where the browser shows a neat table.

Where copy and paste goes wrong, in order of how much it costs: merged cells shift every column to the left in the rows below, and the file looks fine until someone sums the wrong column; numbers with separators and dates convert by the spreadsheet's locale, so day and month can swap silently; links inside cells arrive as text without the address. How to move, and how long it takes

Table Exporter is used at the moment of export, not stored on pages the way a search macro is, so moving is mostly a change of habit. List the reports that depend on it (the monthly budget table, the asset register, the release log), try each one with the replacement, and compare the file with last month's. Check three things in the output: merged cells, dates, and numbers with separators. That is an hour of work, not a project.

The arithmetic that decides whether you need an app at all: one table is two minutes by hand, a page with six tables is fifteen, and a quarterly report pulling tables from forty pages is a working day every quarter. The first two do not need anything.

Frequently asked

Will Table Exporter stop working on 31 January 2027?

That is when Connect support ends, not a switch-off. After it, nothing gets fixed when Confluence changes, and failures pile up. Unless the vendor moves it to Forge, plan to have an alternative tested before then.

Do I lose anything stored on my pages?

No. Your tables are ordinary Confluence content. An exporter reads them; it does not own them. Removing the app leaves every table where it was.

CSV or Excel?

Excel when people will open the file directly: it keeps sheets, and with a good export, numbers and dates stay numbers and dates. CSV when the file feeds another system, which usually wants one plain table.

We are on Data Center. Does this apply?

No. Connect end of support concerns Cloud apps. See what the Connect end of life means for admins.
