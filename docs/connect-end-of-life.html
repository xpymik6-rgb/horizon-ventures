Connect end of life, in plain words

Which of your Confluence apps will stop working, and how to tell.

The short version

Atlassian Cloud apps are built on one of two platforms. Connect is the old one: the app runs on the vendor's own servers and talks to your site over the network. Forge is the new one: the app runs inside Atlassian's own infrastructure.

Atlassian is retiring Connect. Since March 2026 Connect apps can no longer ship updates, and support winds down through 2026 and 2027. Every Connect app either migrates to Forge or dies.

The part that catches people out. Nothing switches off on an announced date. Connect apps degrade: a macro renders on some pages and not others, settings stop saving, then the macro shows an error box. Nobody is notified — you find out when someone needs the page.

How big is this

As of August 2026 the Marketplace lists roughly 6000 cloud apps. About 1475 of them are still on Connect, and 936 of those are paid — which means they have paying customers who will need somewhere to go.

Most of those will migrate. A significant minority will not: their authors moved on years ago, and the app still collects subscriptions without anyone maintaining it.

How to check your own site

1. List what you have

In Confluence: Settings → Manage apps. This gives you every installed app and its vendor.

2. Check each one on the Marketplace

Open the app's Marketplace page. Two things tell you where it stands:

What to look atGood signBad sign
Last release date 2026 2024 or earlier
"Runs on Atlassian" badge Present — it is on Forge, safe Absent and no recent releases
The single most useful signal: a release in 2026. It means somebody is still there. An app last touched in 2023 with no Forge version is not going to be rescued in the time left.

3. Ask the vendor, once

A one-line email — "do you have a Forge migration planned, and when" — sorts the list quickly. No answer within a couple of weeks is itself an answer.

What to do about the ones that are not migrating

  1. Find out how much you actually use it. Confluence advanced search supports macro = "name" — that gives you every page depending on it. Sometimes the answer is four pages and the problem evaporates.
  2. Check what happens to your data. Most apps store content as ordinary page attachments or in the page body — that stays yours regardless. Apps with their own database are the painful case; export before anything breaks.
  3. Replace early, not late. Both apps can be installed at once. Migrating a wiki calmly over a month is a different job from doing it in an afternoon because a diagram everyone needs went blank.

Is Forge actually better, or is this just churn

Honestly, both. But two things genuinely change for you as an admin:

  • Your data stops travelling. A Connect app sends page content to the vendor's servers to do its job. A Forge app carrying the "Runs on Atlassian" badge cannot — Atlassian enforces it at the platform level, not on the vendor's word. For anything under a compliance regime this is the whole argument.
  • Vendor outages stop being your outages. No vendor server in the path means one fewer thing that can be down on a Monday morning.
