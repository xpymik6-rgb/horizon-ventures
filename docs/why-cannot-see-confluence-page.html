Why can't someone see a Confluence page?

You sent a link, they got an error, and you are now the support desk. The answer is almost always one of two layers, and checking them in the wrong order is what turns five minutes into an afternoon.

Short answer. Access is decided in two places: space permissions and page restrictions. Check the space first, because page restrictions can only take access away, never add it. Then check the page, and then check the pages above it, because view restrictions cascade downwards. The list of restricted pages in a space lives in space settings, under Content, in a section called Restricted.

The rule that settles most arguments

Space permissions win. Atlassian spells it out: "if someone lacks space-level permission to edit content, then that person won't be able to edit this content, even though this content's access is set to Open."

Read that twice if you manage a wiki, because it explains the most common failure in access requests. Somebody opens the page, sets access to Open, tells the colleague it is fixed, and the colleague still sees nothing. The page was never the problem. Page restrictions narrow what the space already allows; they cannot widen it.

So the order is not a matter of taste. Space first, page second. Doing it the other way round means you spend your time in the one place that cannot possibly be the cause.

The cascade, and the half of it that does not cascade

View restrictions flow downhill. In Atlassian's words: "If someone can't view a parent content item or folder, they won't be able to view any child content items under it."

This is why the page you are staring at can look completely unrestricted while the person still cannot open it. The restriction sits two levels up, on a parent nobody has thought about since last year, and everything underneath inherits the consequence.

Edit restrictions do not behave the same way: they do not cascade to children. That asymmetry catches people out in the opposite direction. A team locks editing on a section's front page, assumes the whole section is protected, and the pages below stay editable by everyone who has space access.

What this means in practice. When someone reports a page they cannot see, walk up the tree rather than around it. The page itself is the least likely place to find the cause, because if its own restrictions were the problem you would usually have noticed when you set them.

Finding the restrictions without opening every page

There is a list, and most admins never find it because it sits with the other space tools rather than with the page. In space settings, open Content and select Restricted. That gives you the restricted pages in the space in one screen.

It is the same corner of the interface that holds the list of hidden and orphaned items, which is worth knowing in itself: Content is where Confluence keeps the answers to "what is unusual about this space", and almost nobody opens it.

When you are the admin and still cannot get in

Space admins can see what exists in their space and change restrictions on it. That covers the ordinary case: somebody left, their page is locked, you need it back.

The bigger hammer is the admin key, available to organisation admins on Premium and Enterprise, which bypasses restrictions to manage access to any content. If you are on Free or Standard and the only person who could open a page has left the company, that hammer is not in the box, and the honest answer to your colleague is that this is a plan limitation rather than something you are doing wrong.

The five-minute diagnosis

In order, because each step rules out the one before it.

One. Does the person have view permission on the space? If not, stop here and fix that. Nothing on the page matters yet.

Two. Are they in the group you think they are in? Access is usually granted to groups, and "I added them" often means "I added them to a group that does not have this space".

Three. Is the page itself restricted? If viewing is limited to named people, everyone else is out, including colleagues who have full access to the space.

Four. Are any of its parents restricted? This is the step people skip, and it is the one that explains the cases that look impossible.

Five. Open space settings, Content, Restricted, and look at the whole picture. If the same few people appear on every restricted page, you do not have an access problem, you have a habit, and that is a conversation rather than a setting.

Frequently asked

Does sharing a page by link grant access?

No. A link is an address, not a permission. If the two layers above do not allow it, the link produces exactly the error your colleague reported.

Why can they find the page in search but not open it?

Search results and access are different things in several corners of Confluence, which is why a title can surface while the content stays shut. Treat seeing the title as no evidence at all about permissions.

We use restrictions a lot. Is that bad?

It is bad when it is accidental. Restrictions set deliberately on a few sensitive pages are fine; restrictions inherited by a hundred pages because of one forgotten parent are how a wiki quietly becomes unusable.
